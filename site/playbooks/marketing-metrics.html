The 5 Marketing Metrics That Actually Matter

MBRACE Insight playbook

How much did your company spend on marketing last month? Now, how much closed revenue did each channel bring in? If the second answer is a guess, these five numbers fix that.

The five metrics

Metric 1

Cost per lead, by source

spend on a channel ÷ leads from that channel

Useful, but cheap leads aren't always good leads. That's why you need metric 2.

Metric 2

Close rate, by source

jobs closed from a channel ÷ leads from that channel

This shows lead quality. Together with metric 1, it gives you what each customer costs in ad spend alone: cost per lead ÷ close rate.

Metric 3

Customer acquisition cost (CAC), by source

(ad spend + cost of estimates run) ÷ jobs closed

Include the labor of every estimate, for every channel, so the channels are comparable.

Metric 4

Customer lifetime value (LTV), in gross profit

gross profit on the first job + gross profit on future service and repairs

Use gross profit, not revenue. A $12,000 install isn't worth $12,000 to you. It's worth whatever is left after equipment and labor.

Metric 5

Contribution by source

gross profit from a channel's jobs − its ad spend − its estimate costs

This is the number that tells you whether a channel is making money.

How to read it:

  • Facebook has the cheapest leads but the most expensive customers. It still made money in this example ($2,650), so don't kill it on lead cost alone. Test whether changing the offer or targeting improves the close rate.
  • Referrals are the most profitable channel and cost the least. That's a reason to build a deliberate referral program.
  • LSA brings volume at a fair CAC. Look at response speed and service-area ZIPs before changing the budget.
  • Yard signs look strong but on small numbers. Three jobs isn't enough to bet on. Keep tracking.

How much can you spend to win a customer?

IllustrativeA $12,000 install at 45% margin is $5,400 of gross profit. Add a maintenance plan ($200 a year for 10 years at 50% margin, $1,000) and typical repairs ($300 a year for 10 years at 50%, $1,500). LTV ≈ $7,900 in gross profit. A common rule of thumb is to keep CAC well under a third of LTV, here about $2,600, to leave room for overhead and profit.

How to track this without a data analyst

  1. Call tracking: a separate tracking number per channel, all forwarding to your main line. Services like CallRail and CallTrackingMetrics do this; check their current plans.
  2. Source tagging: every call and form gets a source in your CRM, required before the record can be saved.
  3. Estimate tracking: date, source, address, quote amount and status for every estimate.
  4. Revenue attribution: every closed job links back to its original lead source.
  5. A monthly report: the five metrics by source, compared with last month.
