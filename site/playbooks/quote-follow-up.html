You measured the house, explained the options, and left a fair quote. Then nothing. Usually the homeowner didn't choose someone else. They got busy, the numbers blurred together, and the company that stayed in touch won.

Follow-up is not pressure

The difference is intent.

Pressure: "Have you made a decision? This price expires Friday."

Follow-up: "Just checking whether you have questions about what we discussed. Happy to clarify the equipment options or financing."

Not following up isn't respectful. It leaves the homeowner alone with three quotes and a decision they don't fully understand.

Before you text anyoneGet the homeowner's OK to text them at the estimate ("Is it all right if I text you a summary and check in this week?") and note it in your CRM. Stop immediately if anyone asks you to.

The sequence

Day 1 · same day, within 2 hours · text

Thank them and open a direct line

"Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from [Company]. Thanks for meeting with me today. If anything we talked about wasn't clear, you can call or text me directly at this number."

Day 2 · email

Send the financing side by side

"Here's a clearer breakdown of the financing options we discussed. One thing worth saying: if you finance, check your credit union's rates too. Sometimes they beat ours, and I'm happy to work with whatever makes sense for you."

For heat pump jobs, include the rebate the homeowner is applying for and its deadline, with the source and the date you checked it.

Day 3 · phone call

A question-answering call, not a "have you decided" call

"I wanted to check whether you had any questions. HVAC systems can be confusing, and half of what we do is help people understand their options."

If they mention another quote: "Smart to compare. Did they recommend something similar, or a different system? I want to make sure you're comparing the same scope." Don't ask whether they've decided, don't create urgency, don't discount, and don't criticize other companies.

Day 5 · email

Answer the questions homeowners ask after quotes

How long will it last? What happens if something goes wrong? Why are the quotes so different? Should I just go with the cheapest? What if I wait a few months? Short, honest answers, written in your voice.

Day 7 · text

Give them permission to say no

"Just following up one more time. If you went with another company, no hard feelings. Thanks for considering us. If you're still deciding and want to talk anything through, I'm here."

Day 10 · optional final email

Close the file politely

"I haven't heard back, so I'll assume you've gone another way or decided to wait. Both are completely fine. If anything changes, you have my direct number."
