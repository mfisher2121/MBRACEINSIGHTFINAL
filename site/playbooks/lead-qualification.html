The 5-Question Pre-Qualifier

MBRACE Insight playbook

Every in-home estimate costs your team time and drive time. Five questions on the first call help you send the right person to the right home, and give everyone else a useful answer instead of a wasted visit.

This isn't about turning people away. It's about understanding what each caller needs before anyone gets in a truck.

The five questions

Question 1

"What's going on with your system?"

Listen for: an emergency or a planned replacement, specific symptoms, and how long it's been happening.

"Just want a price" or "I'd like a second opinion" are normal. A second opinion is a chance to show how you measure. Ask: "Has anyone been out yet? What did they recommend?"

Question 2

"When are you hoping to get this done?"

Listen for urgency and a decision timeline. Follow up: "If it turns out the system needs replacing, is that something you'd want to move on in that timeframe?"

Question 3

"How old is the current system, and has it been giving you trouble?"

This tells you whether the call is likely a repair or a replacement, and whether the homeowner expects one or the other.

Question 4 · heat pump and replacement jobs

"Has anyone done an energy assessment or a load calculation on the house?"

Homeowners pursuing Maryland's larger utility rebates generally need a home energy assessment first. If they haven't had one, explain the order of work and when an assessment fits. That's often the most useful thing they hear all week.

Question 5

"Do you have a rough budget range in mind?"

"I ask because replacements vary a lot with the size and type of system. I want to make sure our visit is useful for you. Are you also thinking about financing or rebates?"

Give a range only if your company has one it's comfortable stating.

Sort the call

SituationWhat to do
Emergency, or a clear need with a near-term timelineSchedule promptly with the right person.
Real need, flexible timingSchedule at a convenient time; send the follow-up materials.
Early research, or likely a repairOffer a phone conversation, a service call, or helpful material first.
Outside your service area or the kind of work you doSay so honestly and point them to a better fit.

What to say when a visit isn't the right next step

Early research: "Based on what you're describing, I can explain the options over the phone and send you a short guide on what affects price. If it's helpful after that, we'll come out and measure."

Likely a repair: "This sounds like it may be a repair, not a replacement. Let's start with a service call so a technician can diagnose it."

Not your work: "That's outside what we do well. Here's who I'd call instead."

Keep it honestDon't invent a busy schedule to push someone back, and don't talk down about people who shop around. Homeowners compare quotes for good reasons, and how you treat them on the first call is part of your reputation.

Action steps

  1. Print the five questions and keep them by the phone.
  2. Walk your dispatcher through them, along with the sorting table.
  3. For one month, log how each call was sorted and which estimates closed.
  4. Adjust the sorting rules to match your close rates.
